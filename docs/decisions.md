# Decisions

Design decisions with rationale, so the boundary of the prototype is a record, not a gap.
Add a row when a real choice is made. `D#` ids are stable; reference them in commits.

| # | Decision | Rationale | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **No buyer marketplace.** Permanent listing page + per-artisan storefront + a direct structured offer instead. | PS asks for B2B buyers *or* government e-marketplaces — an *or*; the export bundle satisfies the second alone. A catalogue of our own would launch with zero buyers (worst-case cold start). | 30 Aug |
| D2 | **No material classifier.** The material *type* comes from the voice note she already records; the photo only *suggests* category / size class / finish, confirmed by 0–2 spoken questions. | No labelled dataset of Indian craft materials on entry-level phone photos; telling silk from polyester in a dim photo is hard for a person. The artisan is ground truth. | 30 Aug |
| D3 | **Material rates are a dated, sourced snapshot** printed on screen; weekly refresh described, not built. | Only ~5 inputs move enough to matter (brass, copper, silver, silk, cotton); the floor is a warning, not a quote, so staleness barely changes it. | 30 Aug |
| D4 | **Listings carry stock** (unique vs batch + remaining count); reserve-on-accept is the quantity-one case. | An exporter wants 200 diyas, not one; most output is a run, not a single object. | 30 Aug |
| D5 | **Per-artisan storefront** as the year-round channel; the stall QR points at it, not a single listing. | Cheap (aggregation over listing pages) and the largest single change to what a buyer sees; turns a one-day fair visitor into a returning contact. | 30 Aug |
| D6 | **Report-an-issue logs only** — no adjudication, refund, or delist. | Full dispute resolution needs the settlement machinery kept out of scope; a logged, two-sided record is the honest prototype version. | 30 Aug |
| D7 | **Failure-aware image check = mask sanity, not subject contrast.** Retake when coverage is degenerate or the cut-out is fragmented (largest blob < 0.55). | Gating on the subject's internal contrast wrongly rejected legitimately flat-coloured products (a plain pot, a solid dupatta). The real failure is a bad *cut-out*, which shows up in the mask. | 30 Aug (Day 1) |
| D8 | **White balance is scene-based Shades-of-Gray with clamped gains**, not gray-world over the subject. **Revised 11 Sep: colour correction (white balance + CLAHE) is OFF by default** (`image.color_correction: false`); the product keeps the colour it was photographed with, and the pipeline only cuts it out, crops it and puts it on white. | Gray-world over the subject neutralises a strongly single-coloured product — a blue pot came out grey. Estimating the illuminant from the whole frame corrects the cast while keeping the product's colour. **But** the scene estimate fails whenever the *scene* is one colour too: measured 11 Sep on the demo photo `clay-pot.jpg` (orange pot, wooden table, warm background) it applied red ×0.79 / blue ×1.25 and turned terracotta brown (Lab a* +41 → +20, saturation 191 → 154); CLAHE then washed out the sari (L 90 → 105, saturation 166 → 156). Raised by the user ("I want the original colour"). A buyer is buying the colour in the photo, so a wrong correction is worse than none. | 30 Aug (Day 1), revised 11 Sep |
| D9 | **Background removal runs u2netp directly via onnxruntime; the `rembg` wrapper is dropped.** OpenCV pinned to 4.10.x; pip keyring disabled. | rembg imports pymatting+numba whose JIT stalls for minutes on the dev machine; onnxruntime imports in ~0.1s. OpenCV 5.0.0 (beta) imported in 25s; 4.10 in ~1s. pip froze on the macOS Keychain until keyring was disabled. All verified on the target MacBook Air (arm64). | 1 Sep (Day 1) |

| D10 | **Transcription uses `faster-whisper` (CTranslate2) on-device as the default backend**, with an optional whisper.cpp `server` tried first only when `KAARIGAR_WHISPER_SERVER` is set. One `TranscriptResult` type for both. | Actual `whisper.cpp` needs a compiled binary per platform; `faster-whisper` is pip-installable, int8 on CPU, and gives per-segment `avg_logprob` for the confidence gate. Server stays as an online speed/accuracy upgrade without changing callers. | 1 Sep (Day 2) |

| D11 | **No standalone MT model. Gemini (step 3) translates: it takes the regional transcript directly and emits EN + HI.** `translate()` is a passthrough keeping the `TranslationResult` contract; a real IndicTrans2 path exists but only behind `KAARIGAR_MT=indictrans2`. | *Originally:* IndicTrans2-200M via HF `transformers`. **Revised same day:** `import transformers` stalled ~20 min on the anaconda-based venv (it enumerates every distribution on `sys.path`); `torch` is a ~200 MB dep nothing else needs. Second heavy-ML-dep swamp after rembg. Gemini already produces the bilingual text and handles regional input well; a separate MT stage bought little. Offline → transcript passes through untranslated, glossary + read-back still work. | 1 Sep (Day 2) |

| D12 | **Gemini is called over plain REST (`requests`), not the `google-generativeai` SDK.** `describe.py` also does translation (D11). Disk cache at `data/processed/listing_cache/` = the Day-7 pre-cache path. Offline / no key / quota → offline template listing, never a hard failure. | The SDK is deprecated *and* imports in ~156 s on this venv (grpc/proto sys.path scan — the transformers problem again). One `POST` to `generativelanguage.googleapis.com/...:generateContent` needs only `requests` (already a dep). Free-tier model names churn fast (`1.5-flash`→404, `2.5-flash`→"not for new users", `3.6-flash`→needs credits) so the model id is config-driven. | 1 Sep (Day 2) |

| D13 | **The material cost is DERIVED, never asked.** `cost ≈ (material type, from the voice note) × (₹/unit, from the dated rate table) × (size / weight, estimated from photo + voice)`. There is **no spoken "what did the materials cost you" question.** The 0–2 spoken confirmations (D2) are attribute-only — e.g. "brass or bronze?", "cotton or silk?" — and never about price or cost. | The whole problem statement is that a low-literacy artisan can't/shouldn't be asked to price her own work or do cost math — the system decides from one photo + one voice note. Asking her for a rupee figure re-introduces exactly the burden we remove. Everything the estimate needs is already in the photo, the voice note, and the rate table. The fair-price floor stays a *warning* (D3), so an imperfect derived cost never locks her out. | 1 Sep (Day 2) |

| D14 | **Glossary correction is stdlib `difflib`, not a fuzzy-match library; the re-record loop owns no microphone.** `capture()` takes an injected `recorder(prompt_audio, attempt)` callable and keeps the *best* take rather than the last. | Adding `rapidfuzz`/`thefuzz` for ~60 terms is not worth another dependency on a venv that has already been broken twice by installs (watchlist). Keeping mic I/O out of the pipeline means the same loop runs from Flutter, a CLI harness, and the tests — and the tests need no audio. Keeping the best take matters because a retry can come out *worse* (she speaks louder and clips the mic); handing back a worse transcript than we already had would be a regression the artisan can hear in the read-back. | 1 Sep (Day 2) |

| D15 | **The read-back reads back what we HEARD, not the generated listing copy** — her own cleaned transcript, plus any glossary correction and PII redaction spoken aloud, plus the facts we extracted. **Publish requires an explicit spoken yes**; silence, an unclear answer, or an exhausted retry budget all mean no. | Gemini gives us EN + HI only, so a Tamil or Bengali speaker has no listing text in her own language to check — but her *transcript* always is. She is confirming "did you understand me", not proofreading marketing copy, so the transcript is both the language-safe and the more honest thing to verify. Announcing our own edits out loud matters most: silently "fixing" her words is exactly the failure a low-literacy user cannot catch. Defaulting `may_publish` to False means a TTS failure or a walked-away artisan can never publish by accident. | 1 Sep (Day 2) |

| D16 | **The "SHAP three bars" use XGBoost's own `Booster.predict(..., pred_contribs=True)`, not the separate `shap` package.** Grouped into material / demand (category+size+season) / region. Vision does NOT propose a specific category name (e.g. "clay pot") — only a `size_class` and a `finish`, both from cheap real pixel signals (bbox coverage; specular-highlight spread in the subject mask). Category comes from the voice transcript (already the case via `describe.py`). | For a tree ensemble, XGBoost's native contribution output IS the exact Shapley-value decomposition, not an approximation — so nothing is lost by skipping `shap`, which took ~20s just to `import` on this machine (measured 5 Sep), the same class of import-time tax D9/D11/D12 already refused (rembg, transformers, the Gemini SDK). Separately: naming a specific category from shape alone with no trained classifier would be a guess dressed up as a vision finding — the same reasoning D2 already used to refuse a material classifier applies here too, so category suggestion is deliberately left out of vision's scope. | 5 Sep (Day 4) |

| D17 | **Backend DB is SQLite (`backend/data/kaarigar.db`), not the architecture doc's PostgreSQL**, via the same SQLAlchemy ORM code — swappable later with only a `DATABASE_URL` env var change, no code migration. The one caveat: `stock.py`'s `.with_for_update()` row lock is a real lock on Postgres but a no-op on SQLite; SQLite's own whole-database write serialization still prevents a double-accept at dev/demo scale, but this is not the same guarantee a real row lock gives under concurrent writers. | No Postgres instance installed on the dev machine and Day 5 needed a working DB today, not a provisioning detour. The schema and query code are Postgres-ready; moving there before a real multi-writer demo (Day 7 hardening) is a config change, not a rewrite. Disclosed here rather than left as a silent substitution. | 6 Sep (Day 5) |
| D18 | **OTP delivery is mocked**: `/onboarding/send_otp` generates a real 4-digit code with a real 5-minute server-side expiry, but returns it directly in the response instead of dispatching an SMS (no SMS provider budgeted). The app speaks the code aloud via TTS immediately — the same UX a real SMS-read-aloud integration would have, just without the carrier hop. | No SMS provider (Twilio/etc.) key or budget in the roadmap's cut list. Faking "she received a text" would hide a real integration gap; returning the OTP in-band and disclosing it here keeps the shortcut honest and the swap-in point (`send_otp`'s one function body) obvious. | 6 Sep (Day 5) |
| D19 | **The suggested price is editable, not final.** A new step in `publish_screen.dart` — prefilled with the model's suggestion, changeable via the same numeric keypad used elsewhere — sits between verification and stock type. Going below the known floor shows a plain warning (material+labour cost) but never blocks; there's no upper bound either. | The pricing model (Day 4) was always designed as advisory — the wireframe itself says the floor "warns without seizing her decision" and the band is "never a single false-precision number" — but until 6 Sep the app quietly published at the exact suggested number with no way to change it, contradicting that. Raised by the user during real-device testing ("pricing model is just a suggestive measure, we wanna allow user to input his desired price"); not on any roadmap day, but consistent with the existing design intent rather than a new one. | 6 Sep (pre-Day 6) |

| D20 | **The weekly follower digest is assembled for real but never actually sent.** `POST /storefront/follow` stores a real `Follower` row with a real one-click unsubscribe token, and `GET /storefront/{id}/digest_preview` builds the exact content a weekly send would carry (new listings in the last 7 days, recipient count). No SMTP dispatch. | No email provider is budgeted, the same gap D18 discloses for SMS. Building the *content* path for real means the only missing piece is the transport — a demo can honestly show "this is what her followers get" without claiming email that doesn't leave the machine. Faking a sent-mail confirmation would hide a real integration gap; a preview endpoint labels it. | 7 Sep (Day 6) |
| D21 | **A listing can be renamed by voice OR by typing, both offered on one screen**, before publish (from `PublishScreen`) and after (from `DashboardScreen`). The text field is the only keyboard surface in the entire artisan-facing app. | Raised by the user ("allow user to edit name of the listing before listing and also after"). Asked whether it should be voice-only — consistent with every other artisan-facing input, where numbers use a custom keypad and everything text-like is spoken — or a plain text field; the answer was **both**. Voice stays the primary affordance (big mic button, matching the rest of the app) and typing is the alternative for anyone who can write a short name, rather than assuming every artisan either can or can't. The deliberate exception is recorded here so a future reader doesn't "fix" it back to voice-only by accident. | 7 Sep (Day 6) |
| D22 | **The export bundle is a structured data HANDOFF (an `.xlsx` of GeM/ONDC/Amazon-Karigar/ODOP-style catalog columns), not a live marketplace integration** — and fields this prototype genuinely doesn't collect (GSTIN, HSN code, bank account) are written as explicit "not collected — …" strings, never left blank. | The user asked directly whether it would be wiser to just let her sell on Amazon/Flipkart/Zepto instead. It isn't buildable: Amazon SP-API and Flipkart's seller API both require an already-approved, KYC'd seller account (GSTIN, PAN, bank account, business entity) — a weeks-to-months onboarding no app can shortcut, which is *why* Amazon Karigar exists as a separate assisted programme in the first place. A "sell on Amazon" button that didn't create a real Amazon listing would misrepresent the prototype to a judging panel. Extends D1's reasoning from "no marketplace of our own" to "no faked integration with someone else's". Marking uncollected fields explicitly keeps the sheet honest about what a human still has to fill in. | 7 Sep (Day 6) |

| D23 | **The cut-out's soft edge is tightened and recoloured from the product** (`image.edge_cleanup`, on by default): the mask's ramp is contrast-stretched to ~1 px of anti-aliasing, and every part-transparent pixel takes its colour from the nearest fully-opaque product pixels instead of from the photo. A fully-opaque pixel is never touched, so D8's promise is unchanged. | u2netp decides the mask at 320×320 and it is stretched back up, so the edge arrives as a wide ramp, not a boundary — measured 11 Sep: **5.0 px on `clay-pot.jpg`, 8.2 px on `saree.jpeg`**, then upscaled again by `_crop_to_square` on the way to 1000 px. Compositing that ramp straight onto white keeps the old background's colour in every part-transparent pixel (saturation in the band fell to 54 against the pot's own 193, and 46 against the saree's 174) — that band *is* the faint grey rim. It also matters for colour: on a listing thumbnail a rim that wide reads as the whole product being paler than the photo, which is the "it doesn't look the same" complaint even when the interior is bit-identical. After the fix the rim is **0.8 px / 1.1 px**, its colour moves back toward the product's, and the core's ΔBGR against the photo is **0**. | 11 Sep (Day 7) |

| D24 | **The enhanced image is never enlarged** — `_crop_to_square` scales down to 1000 px and otherwise keeps the crop's own pixels, so `enhanced.png` is square but not always 1000 px. Same rule the texture close-up already follows. | It resized to 1000 px unconditionally with `INTER_AREA`, which is a *downscaling* filter: asked to enlarge, OpenCV steps it like nearest-neighbour. A product filling a third of the frame leaves a crop far under 1000 px — `clay-pot.jpg` gave a 356 px crop enlarged 2.81×, landing up to **105 levels per pixel** away from a proper Lanczos enlargement, which is what put staircase edges on the pot's gradient and the saree's motifs. Enlarging invents no detail, so the choice is between smooth-but-fake and honest-and-sharp; the close-up settled the same question the same way on 11 Sep. A real phone capture (1600 px after the app's upload downscale) usually still hits the downscale path, so this mostly changes small or zoomed-out photos. Consumers already resize whatever they get (share card, `services/photos.py`, the listing page's `object-fit`), and a browser enlarging a small image uses a smooth filter rather than the baked-in steps. | 11 Sep (Day 7) |
